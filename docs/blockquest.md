# BlockQuest

Open My games → Choose a game → BlockQuest on an existing multiple-choice question set. Choose Smartboard for two teams, or Single-player mission for teacher practice. Test smartboard · two teams and Test single-player open isolated practice runs with fictional learners and no marks saved. A Test smartboard link is also available on the game setup screen. Test runs never replace an ongoing class game. Learners find BlockQuest in the existing Play menu after joining their assigned question set.

On desktop smartboards, tall playfields sit on the left and right. The question and controls occupy the bottom centre, expanding upward for longer prompts and choices without overlapping either playfield. Smaller screens retain the stacked layout.

Smartboard shuffles the selected roster into two teams, alternates team turns and cycles through every member of a team before reshuffling. Without a roster, it uses anonymous players and does not record learner marks. Single-player learner sessions are private to the game and authenticated learner; teacher single-player previews do not save learner marks.

New games start a 20-second countdown as soon as the learner name and question appear. The question alone is read automatically using the existing natural Marin narration service; choices are never narrated automatically. A missed deadline records an incorrect answer. Incorrect answers show the answer and explanation for 2.5 seconds, then automatically call the next learner. A correct answer earns 100 points and immediately unlocks three distinct shapes for a 15-second building turn. Choose, rotate and move a block using touch or keyboard (left/right, up to rotate, Enter to place). During questions, only the large countdown is shown; one louder beep per second warns from 10 seconds remaining. Sound off silences these warnings. After a correct answer the selected block falls from the top, one row per 1.5 seconds; shape changes and steering must fit at its current height. The ghost previews its landing. Drop block places it immediately; collision or the building deadline also places it automatically. Placed blocks earn 10 game points and each full row earns 250. Clearing multiple rows adds 150 points for each pair of rows cleared together (two rows: 660 total; three: 1210; four: 1910, before chain bonuses). Matching smartboard team turns receive the same three-shape offer, saved across reloads and tied to turn number even if a team answers incorrectly. A normal placement takes one second; row clears take 3.4 seconds for a neon charge, shattering blocks, particles, settling, a points burst and rocket construction before the next turn opens automatically. Teacher pause freezes answer, falling and scene clocks. Incorrect answers reveal the correct answer and explanation without awarding a block. Music reuses the bundled ColonyQuest soundtrack, with local building, success, row-clear and countdown effects. The neon arena uses cyan and pink team borders and illuminated blocks. A Web Audio analyser measures only the playing soundtrack to drive a smoothed ambient glow, sampling music at 20 Hz and easing its display each frame. Glow stops for muted music, paused or ended games, hidden tabs and reduced-motion preferences; narration and effects do not drive it. Voice, music and effects have separate controls. Browser audio requires the initial Start/Resume interaction. The highest combined answer and building score wins. Existing saved games retain their original manual pacing; start a new game to use timers. If none of the offered shapes can fit, a rescue clears the bottom three rows without awarding points. Each team builds a rocket in three earned stages: boosters, cabin and nose. The third row completes a short launch. Rescue removals never advance this mission. Solo ends on three cleared rows or the last question; smartboard ends on the last question or teacher Finish. Teacher pause and refresh resume are supported. Returning teachers can also choose Restart game beside Resume saved game. Confirmation replaces the unfinished run with a fresh question-one game and matching starter rows; previously completed results remain unchanged. Learners cannot restart an unfinished run.

Sessions and original question snapshots are persisted under DATA_DIR/blockquest. The server validates identity, phase, revision, answer choice and placement. Answer keys are not sent ahead of feedback. Earned game points are separate from correct/attempted learning results. Named learner evidence uses original question indices and idempotent run/student result IDs. Completion integrates with the existing game results and gradebook pipeline.

Validation: node --test tests/blockquest*.test.js; npx playwright test tests/e2e/blockquest.spec.js --project=windows-100 --project=mobile. Browser checks cover smartboard and solo flows, replay protection, resume, learner-only control boundaries, private sessions and persisted learning results. Classroom trials and direct smartboard hardware testing are still needed before claiming classroom readiness.

Class duels on learner devices are documented in [blockquest-duels.md](blockquest-duels.md). They reuse the same turn rules, neon visuals, soundtrack and question narration.

## Immersive worlds
All BlockQuest clients share `blockquest-world.js` and its stylesheet. Original procedural Aurora, Ember city and Stardust environments change every three rows (and each duel round); Scenery lets the teacher or learner pin a world. The cached skyline, bounded particle budget, capped pixel density and adaptive renderer (60 fps target, reducing particles before dropping to 30 fps) keep effects independent of gameplay. Existing soundtrack amplitude drives glow; there is no flashing full-screen inversion. Clear bursts originate at the active tower and are clipped away from reading surfaces. Pause/hidden tabs stop the ambient loop. Reduced motion or Gentle effects uses static scenery. Question-answering never shows an unearned falling block.

### Smooth motion
`blockquest-motion.js` keeps earned pieces in one composited layer, interpolates falling against the authoritative 1.5-second row clock, eases sideways moves and rotations, and starts hard drops from the displayed position. It does not change question/build deadlines, collision rules or marks. Unchanged boards survive polling; opponent updates do not replace the learner’s tower. Scenery uses one continuous clock with preserved quality settings; music is sampled at 20 Hz and its displayed glow eased each frame. Pause, tab hiding and reduced motion remain respected.

Smartboard foreground: after a correct answer, the active team’s tower moves into the main playing position with block controls beside it. The other team becomes a small background tower until the turn changes. Questions retain the shared reading layout; single-player layout is unaffected.

## Reward and battle rhythm
New board/solo runs and duel rounds use a shared starter row with a four-cell gap and an opening I/O/T offer. Correct answers still earn 100 arcade points; a placement earns 10, every cleared row 250, a multi-row clear adds 150 per pair, and successive clears add 50/100/150 chain points. Quiz learning marks remain answer-based. Starter blocks (B) and cracked attack blocks (R) use normal placement/clear rules.

Smartboard clears attack the other team automatically; duel clears attack the paired opponent. Each clear first cancels queued incoming rows. Pending plus existing attack rows are capped at two; incoming rows wait for the next correct answer/build and are blocked automatically on crowded towers. Single-player has no invented opponent: clears advance its rocket and trigger the same stronger celebrations. Existing saved towers remain intact; restart or start a new run for the starter setup.

The smartboard's last quarter (up to four turns), the solo rocket approaching completion, and duels' final 30 seconds build music energy. The existing track preserves pitch while rising to 1.12x tempo, with a soft bass beat. Narration ducks its volume. No extra menus, answer decisions or opponent waits are introduced.
